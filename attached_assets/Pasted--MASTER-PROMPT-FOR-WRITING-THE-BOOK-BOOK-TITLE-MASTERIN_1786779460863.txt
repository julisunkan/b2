# CORE WRITING PRINCIPLES

Follow these principles throughout the entire manuscript.

### 1. Be educational

Explain the "why" behind techniques rather than simply giving instructions.

For example, don't merely say:

"Brown the chicken."

Explain why browning develops flavor, what temperature is appropriate, what visual cues indicate proper browning, and what mistakes to avoid.

### 2. Be practical

Readers must be able to actually cook the recipes.

Give:

* Precise ingredient quantities
* Preparation times
* Cooking times
* Temperatures where relevant
* Number of servings
* Clear step-by-step instructions
* Equipment requirements where necessary
* Troubleshooting advice
* Storage instructions
* Serving suggestions

### 3. Preserve culinary authenticity

Respect the culinary traditions of France and Spain.

Do not arbitrarily rename dishes, alter traditional recipes, or present modern substitutions as authentic.

When a recipe has multiple regional or historical variations, explain this clearly.

Distinguish between:

* Traditional version
* Common modern variation
* Optional adaptation

### 4. Avoid stereotypes

Do not reduce French cuisine to baguettes, cheese, wine, and butter or Spanish cuisine to paella and tapas.

Represent the regional diversity of both countries.

### 5. Use natural, engaging prose

The writing should feel like a knowledgeable chef personally guiding the reader through the kitchen.

Avoid repetitive phrases such as:

"Get ready to embark on a culinary journey."

Avoid excessive promotional language.

Avoid unnecessary filler.

### 6. Do not plagiarize

All explanations, introductions, descriptions, recipe instructions, stories, and educational material must be written originally.

Do not copy recipes or passages from existing cookbooks or websites.

Common culinary facts may be explained in original language.

### 7. Do not invent historical facts

When discussing the origins of dishes, ingredients, techniques, or culinary traditions:

* Use established historical knowledge.
* Clearly distinguish documented history from culinary folklore.
* Never fabricate dates, people, restaurants, or historical events.
* If a precise historical claim is uncertain, phrase it cautiously.

---

# TARGET BOOK LENGTH

Aim for approximately:

**80,000–110,000 words**

Target approximately:

**250–350 pages**, depending on typography, images, recipe formatting, and page size.

Include approximately:

**120–150 recipes**

Divide the recipes reasonably between French and Spanish cuisine.

Do not artificially increase word count.

Quality is more important than length.

# INTENDED READER

Write for an international English-speaking audience.

Assume the reader may not understand French or Spanish culinary terminology.

Whenever an unfamiliar culinary term is introduced:

1. Give the original term.
2. Give the English meaning.
3. Explain what it means in practical cooking terms.

Example:

**Mirepoix** — a mixture of diced onion, carrot, and celery used as a foundational flavor base in many French preparations.

# RECIPE DEVELOPMENT RULES

Every recipe must follow this exact general structure.

## RECIPE TITLE

Include the original French or Spanish name where appropriate.

### Introduction

Write a concise paragraph explaining:

* What the dish is
* Where it belongs in the cuisine
* Why it is important or enjoyable
* Any relevant regional information

### Recipe Information

**Prep Time:**
**Cook Time:**
**Total Time:**
**Servings:**
**Difficulty:**
**Cuisine:**

### Equipment

List only equipment genuinely required.

### Ingredients

Give precise quantities.

Use both metric and common U.S. measurements where practical.

Example:

**250 g (8.8 oz) flour**

For liquids:

**240 ml (1 cup) milk**

Do not use vague quantities such as "some flour" or "a little salt" unless there is a legitimate culinary reason.

### Instructions

Use numbered steps.

Each step should contain enough information for a beginner to understand what to do.

Include visual or sensory cues where useful:

* "until golden brown"
* "until the onions are translucent"
* "until the sauce coats the back of a spoon"

Avoid unnecessarily precise cooking times when visual cues are more reliable.

### Chef's Tips

Give 2–5 useful professional tips.

### Common Mistakes

List the most likely mistakes and how to prevent them.

### Variations

Give appropriate variations where they make culinary sense.

Do not create arbitrary variations simply to increase word count.

### Substitutions

Mention practical alternatives when appropriate.

### Serving Suggestions

Explain how the dish can be served.

### Storage

Explain:

* Refrigeration
* Freezing where appropriate
* Reheating
* Recommended storage duration where reasonably established

Do not give unsafe food-storage advice.

### Cultural Note

Include a short cultural or regional note when appropriate.

# RECIPE QUALITY CONTROL

Before accepting each recipe, internally check:

1. Are all ingredients used?
2. Are ingredient quantities plausible?
3. Are cooking times realistic?
4. Are temperatures appropriate?
5. Does the recipe produce the intended dish?
6. Are the instructions logically ordered?
7. Are there missing preparation steps?
8. Is the recipe technically feasible in a normal home kitchen?
9. Are substitutions sensible?
10. Is the dish being represented accurately?
11. Are safety considerations adequately addressed?
12. Is the recipe sufficiently distinct from other recipes?

Never knowingly publish a recipe with contradictory quantities or instructions.
